# From phase tendencies to harvest and production risk

This account follows the scientific logic of the study, not a dated experiment log.

## From ENSO phase associations to individual harvests

Phase averages establish where yields have historically been higher or lower. Comparing shared physical events asks how often those tendencies describe an actual harvest. Event omission and contrary-event records expose both recurring information and important exceptions ([Figures 1–2](figures/README.md)). Retrospective direction agreement and magnitude deviations answer different questions.

## Differences among agricultural environments

Climate background, growing-season length and irrigation characterize farming settings without defining groups from yield outcomes. Soybean and wheat comparisons examine whether similar ENSO phases or strengths accompany different yield distributions in these settings. Five-weather models describe fitted associations and their historical allocation across factors; the country and production summaries can differ ([Figure 3](figures/main/Figure_03.png)).

## Connecting yield anomalies to production

Individual events reveal differences among countries and crops experiencing the same ENSO background. Weather totals and within-season variation supply different information. The production analysis then asks how local yield departures combine across covered producers, allowing gains elsewhere and harvested-area changes ([Figures 4–5](figures/README.md)). Counting low-yield countries cannot replace reported-production accounting.

## Conditional harvest risk

The dated ENSO outlook available by 8 November 2026 is combined with historical yield distributions only for systems meeting timing and data-support conditions. The 2027 scenarios distinguish absolute severe-low-yield risk, its change from a historical baseline and the production represented by eligible systems. They preserve incomplete phase-probability allocation as bounds rather than selecting an arbitrary midpoint ([Figure 6](figures/main/Figure_06.png)).

## What the evidence supports

The study connects historical phase information with contrary harvests, agricultural settings and production geography. Its comparisons are observational; weather-model components describe fitted relationships. Neither those components nor the event comparisons independently identify ENSO-caused losses. The dated scenarios support research interpretation and monitoring priorities within eligible coverage, rather than validated local harvest forecasts or global loss estimates.
