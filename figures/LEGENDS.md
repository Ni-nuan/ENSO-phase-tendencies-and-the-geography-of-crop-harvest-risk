**Fig. 1 | Historical ENSO phase–yield differences and individual event harvests across four major crops.** **a,b,d,e,g,h,j,k**, Differences in mean annual yield anomalies between El Niño or La Niña and majority-neutral years for maize, rice, soybean and wheat (percentage points, pp; 694 eligible country–crop–phase comparisons). Blue indicates higher and red lower mean yields relative to majority-neutral years; grey denotes insufficient or withheld support. Upward and downward triangles indicate positive and negative differences. **c,f,i,l**, Mean yield anomalies (%) across 25 physical ENSO events for the five largest producers of each crop, identified using 2012–2020 reference production. Events are ordered by onset; column labels indicate onset year and phase (E, El Niño; L, La Niña). Black dots indicate negative anomalies. Crossed producer labels indicate unresolved growing-season calendars; grey cells lack qualified event outcomes. Annual anomalies use centred five-year geometric means. The maps show phase-minus-neutral differences, whereas matrices show event means. Their colour scales span ±20 pp and ±30%, respectively.

**Fig. 2 | Historical ENSO tendencies, contrary harvests and geographic variability.** **a**, Yield-direction agreement with held-out events using histories from the same phase (filled crop symbols) or all other events (open grey circles), across 5,541 system–event records from 376 systems. Lines connect paired event-equal summaries. **b**, Counts of 276 country–crop–phase contrasts with mean differences of at least 2 pp that retained direction and eligibility after each complete-event omission. Orange denotes histories with an event mean at least 2 pp from the majority-neutral mean in the opposite direction; grey denotes histories without one. **c**, Counts of 319 dual-phase systems by severe-low-yield frequency relative to majority-neutral years. Severe low yield is an annual anomaly ≤−10% against the centred five-year reference. The outlined cell identifies increased frequency in both phases. **d**, Spearman correlations between country yield rankings in held-out events and other same-phase histories (92 eligible event comparisons). Points represent comparisons; boxes span the interquartile range, centre lines indicate medians, diamonds indicate means and whiskers span the observed range. n denotes comparisons within each crop.

**Fig. 3 | Yield distributions and weather contributions across agricultural environments.** **a,b**, El Niño-minus-majority-neutral differences in mean yield (a) and severe-low-yield frequency (b), in pp, for 11 cooler, longer-season and 23 warmer soybean systems. Severe low yield is an annual anomaly ≤−10%. **c**, Strong-minus-ordinary La Niña yield differences for 39 cooler, less-irrigated and 29 warmer, drier, more-irrigated wheat systems. In a–c and g, points represent systems, boxes span interquartile ranges, centre lines indicate medians, diamonds indicate means and whiskers span observed ranges. **d**, Relative contributions of five weather variables by crop and phase across 459 systems. Values are percentages of their summed mean absolute SHAP contributions. VPD, vapour pressure deficit. **e**, Equal-system changes in contribution shares, 2002–2022 minus 1984–2001 (pp), for balanced cohorts of 126 maize, 100 rice, 68 soybean and 91 wheat systems. **f**, Soybean VPD and soil-moisture share changes on the same 68 systems under equal-country weighting (open circles) and fixed production weighting (filled diamonds; 81.36% reference world-production coverage). Lines connect weighting alternatives. **g**, Early-to-late changes in the VPD-minus-soil-moisture share gap for separate wheat cohorts of 25 cooler and 26 warmer systems sharing four strong La Niña events. **h**, Strong-minus-ordinary La Niña contrasts (pp) in observed yield, signed five-weather contributions and full fitted outputs for the cohorts in g. The cohorts in c and g,h have different eligibility. Panel d shows relative contribution shares (%); e–g show historical changes in shares or share gaps (pp).

**Fig. 4 | Observed yield anomalies and modelled weather contributions during ENSO events.** **a**, Observed event-mean yield anomalies (%) and summed signed SHAP contributions from five weather variables (pp), for 1,804 records from 116 systems sharing 25 physical ENSO events: maize, 524; rice, 414; soybean, 432; wheat, 434. Hollow crop symbols mark coordinate-wise medians; horizontal and vertical segments span the respective interquartile ranges. Unfilled ellipses are centred on these medians, use robust covariance and enclose approximately 75% of each crop’s records. **b**, Argentine and South African maize yield anomalies across eight shared El Niño events, including the labelled 1993 harvest. **c**, United States maize and soybean yield anomalies across 13 shared events with matched harvest years (seven El Niño and six La Niña). Dashed diagonals in b,c indicate equal yield anomalies. **d–f**, Paired harvests for Lesotho maize (1998 and 2016), South African maize (1993 and 1995) and Australian wheat (2007 and 2010). Blue circles show observed anomalies (%), green squares full fitted anomalies (%) and purple triangles signed five-weather contributions (pp). Lines connect discrete harvests. The weather contribution sums the model components for precipitation, temperature, VPD, soil moisture and shortwave radiation; the full output also includes system, trend and non-weather terms.

**Fig. 5 | Yield anomalies, harvested area and reported production among major producers.** **a**, Counts of low-yield (anomaly ≤−2%) and high-yield (≥+2%) producers within 150 crop–event–harvest-year groups. Bubble area represents the number of groups. Blue identifies the 79 groups with at least two low-yield producers; grey identifies the remaining groups. **b**, Combined yield contributions at fixed area and reported-production anomalies for the same groups, expressed relative to their common yield–area reference. The dashed diagonal indicates equality; solid lines mark zero and dotted lines mark −2%. **c,d**, Maize production accounting for 1988 and 2000, covering 63.33% and 10.23% of actual world production, respectively. First and second crops are defined separately for each pair. **f**, Reported-production outcomes for the 45 pairs with yield anomalies ≤−2% in both crops. Low production denotes an anomaly ≤−2%. Yield and reported-production anomalies in e,f use their respective historical references.

**Fig. 6 | Conditional severe-low-yield risks for 2027 harvests under the ENSO outlook.** **a–d**, Risk changes from the all-history baseline (pp), using outlook information available on 8 November 2026, for maize (52 systems), rice (41), soybean (24) and wheat (83). Severe low yield is ≤−10% relative to the scenario trend reference. Production coverage uses fixed reference world shares. Blue denotes lower and red higher risk. Grey shades distinguish excluded systems as labelled; white indicates no candidate. Triangles indicate risk direction, diamonds incomplete phase-probability allocation and open circles unchanged risk without allocation uncertainty. **e**, All 200 system risk changes. Boxes span the 25th–75th percentiles, centre lines indicate medians and whiskers span the observed range. Map colours, points and boxes use the allocation endpoint nearest zero, or zero when bounds span zero. Thin coloured segments show system bounds. Paired open black diamonds mark lower and upper fixed-production-weighted changes within eligible coverage; narrow ranges may overlap. The weighted ranges are maize, −5.46 to −5.45 pp; rice, −0.07 to +0.19 pp; soybean, −1.76 to −1.75 pp; and wheat, −0.80 to −0.71 pp. For maize, rice, soybean and wheat, respectively, system counts in the higher/lower/unchanged/unresolved categories are 22/25/3/2, 18/15/6/2, 10/12/2/0 and 43/38/2/0. The interval endpoints need not be attainable together under a single season-consistent phase-probability allocation. Full distribution summaries are in Supplementary Table 13. **f**, Historical baseline risks (open circles) and scenario risks (blue points or capped intervals) for eight systems. Grey dashed lines connect each baseline to the scenario’s lower endpoint; blue capped lines show probability-allocation bounds. Indonesian rice’s baseline and scenario marks overlap at zero. Allocation bounds reflect incomplete phase probabilities and are distinct from confidence intervals. Probability changes describe risk across covered production, rather than changes in production tonnage.
